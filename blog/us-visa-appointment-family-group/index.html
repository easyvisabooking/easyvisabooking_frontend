Why a group is a harder problem, not a bigger one

Most people assume a family booking is the same task repeated. It is not. Grouped applicants are moved together, so the system needs enough capacity on one date to take the whole party.

Think about where earlier appointments come from at a busy post. Almost all of them are cancellations: one applicant releases one slot, and it returns to the pool. Those arrive singly, at unpredictable moments, all day long. A solo applicant can take any of them. A family of four needs four to be available at the same moment on the same date, which is a far less frequent event than four times as likely.

Two practical consequences follow, and they are the whole strategy for a group:

  • A wide acceptable date range matters more for you than for anyone else. A solo applicant hunting one specific week has a hard problem. A family hunting one specific week has a much harder one. Widening the window is the only lever that meaningfully changes the odds.
  • Bulk releases matter more to you than cancellations do. When a post loads a new block of dates, multiple slots appear at once, which is the realistic moment for a group to move. Where a mission publishes its release window, that window is worth far more to a family than to an individual.

Grouped or separate: the real trade-off

Applicants ask whether they should book the family together or separately, and the honest answer is that it depends on whether anyone in the party has a different deadline.

For most families the grouped booking is right, and the loss of individual flexibility is a fair price for arriving together. The case for separating is narrow and specific: one traveller has a fixed date that the rest do not, for a conference, a course or a treatment, and the family's convenience is worth less than that person's timing.

Splitting a group, if you have to

Where applicants share a scheduling account, the portal will often not offer an individual change at all. That is deliberate rather than a fault, and it means splitting is a support request rather than something you can do yourself.

  1. Do not try to force it in the portal. A failed attempt can still consume a reschedule attempt, and you may end up with fewer options than you started with.
  2. Contact the official scheduling support channel for your country, reached from your embassy or consulate's own website rather than from search results.
  3. Ask before you need it. Establishing whether a split is possible for your case takes a few days and is much cheaper to do while your existing appointment is still comfortably ahead of you.
  4. Understand what it costs. Depending on the platform, separating applicants can mean new bookings from current availability, which at a busy post may be worse than the date you already hold.

Do not break a working family booking to chase a better date

This is the mistake we see and it is not recoverable. A family holds a confirmed appointment eleven months out, decides to split so one person can move earlier, and ends up with a partial booking and a calendar that has nothing for the rest. Whatever you are considering, keep the confirmed group date intact until something better is actually in hand. The date you hold is worth more than the plan you are imagining.

The per-person biometrics check

In countries that use a separate biometrics or document collection appointment, the rule that each person's biometrics must precede their interview, generally by a day or two, applies individually.

So when a group interview moves earlier, the check is not one check. Look at every member of the party and confirm that each biometrics date still sits comfortably before the new interview date. One person out of order is enough to cause a cancellation, and it is exactly the thing that gets missed when you are pleased about the new date and moving quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Can a family get US visa appointments on the same day?

Yes, and in most cases that is how the system is designed to work. Applicants grouped in one scheduling account are booked and moved together, which keeps a family on the same date without you having to coordinate it. The difficulty is not getting the same day, it is getting an earlier same day at a busy post.

Why is it harder to reschedule a family than one person?

Because the group moves as a unit, so you need enough openings on a single date for everybody. At busy posts the main supply of earlier appointments is individual cancellations arriving one at a time, and those rarely appear in twos and threes on the same day. A family of four is looking for a much rarer event than a solo applicant is.

Can I reschedule just one person out of a group?

Usually not through the portal. Where applicants share a scheduling account the system moves them together and an individual change is often not offered at all. Splitting a group is a support request rather than a self-service action, and attempting to force it can still consume one of your reschedule attempts.

Should I book my family separately to keep flexibility?

It is a real trade-off rather than an obvious yes. Separate bookings give each applicant independent flexibility, which makes earlier dates easier to find. They also create the risk of the family ending up on different days at different times, more appointments to keep track of, and separate reschedule allowances to manage. For most families the grouped booking is the right default, and the exception is where one member has a hard deadline the others do not.

Do children need their own appointment and their own fees?

Yes. Each applicant needs their own DS-160 and their own application fee, and the $250 visa integrity fee where the post is collecting it applies per person as well. Nothing in this process has a family rate. A family of four looking at a B-1/B-2 application is $740 in application fees and $1,000 in integrity fees before anything else.

How does the $750 expedite work for a family?

Per applicant, which is what makes it hard for families. At Mission Mexico or Mission Canada, where the pilot runs, a family of four is $3,000 in expedite fees on top of $740 in application fees. It is non-refundable, so a trip that falls through takes the money with it. For a family the arithmetic on rescheduling is very different from the arithmetic for one traveller.

Does the biometrics appointment rule apply to each person?

Yes, where your country uses a separate biometrics or document collection appointment. Each applicant's biometrics has to sit before the interview, generally by at least a day or two, so the data is processed and available to the consular officer. When you move a group interview earlier, check every member's biometrics date, because one person out of order can cause a cancellation.

The bottom line

A group booking makes arriving together easy and makes moving earlier hard. The system moves you as a unit, so you are waiting for a date with room for everybody rather than for any opening at all, and single cancellations do not provide that.

Three things follow. Widen your acceptable date range further than you would as a solo applicant, because it is the only lever that changes the odds materially. Pay attention to bulk releases rather than cancellations, and to any release window your mission publishes, because that is when multiple slots appear at once. And check every family member's biometrics date whenever the interview moves.

Finally, price the whole thing before you decide anything. Four people is $1,740 in government fees before travel, and $3,000 more if you use the paid expedite where it exists. Those numbers change which option makes sense.

What nobody can promise you

Nobody outside the US government can guarantee an interview slot, a timeframe, or a visa decision. We cannot either, and for a family we will say plainly that the event we are waiting for is rarer than the one a solo applicant needs. What we offer is that the work gets done properly and that you pay only if it produces a result.

The Complete US Visa Appointment Guide: Booking, Moving and Understanding Your Date

Everything that decides when you get an interview, in the order it actually happens, with the current 2026 rules on portals, fees, expedites and where you are allowed to apply. Written for someone who has just discovered the calendar is empty.

Step 1: form, fee, and the portal you are actually on

Before anything can be booked you need a submitted DS-160 and a recognised application fee payment. Two things about this stage cost people real time.

The DS-160 cannot be edited after submission. Correcting an error means a new form, which generates a new confirmation number, and your appointment is tied to the old one. That is an administrative problem rather than a refusal risk, and at a post with a long queue it is the expensive kind. Spend the extra ten minutes checking the passport number, the date of birth, the SEVIS ID and the photograph upload before you submit. The detail is in DS-160 mistakes that delay your appointment.

Countries do not share one scheduling platform. India, Canada and many others use the AIS portal at ais.usvisa-info.com. The UAE and others moved to usvisascheduling.com in 2025. Screens, wording and reschedule allowances differ, so a walkthrough written for one will not match the other. Reach your scheduling site by following the link from your own embassy or consulate's website, every time.

Bank payments and cash deposits can take one to two business days to be recognised. Until they are, you cannot schedule anything, and the screen will not explain why.

Step 2: book immediately, whatever the date

This is the single most important instruction in this guide, and the one most often ignored.

Take the first date the system offers you. If it is fourteen months out, take it. A confirmed appointment is not a resignation, it is a position:

  • You cannot reschedule what you do not hold. The reschedule function acts on an existing booking and does not appear without one.
  • You cannot request an expedite without an existing booking. Consular sections consider a request only once you hold an appointment.
  • You cannot use the $750 lane without an existing booking either, where it applies. It is an upgrade to a booking, not a way to book from scratch.

Every day spent researching before booking is a day added to the end of your timeline, and the calendar does not improve while you read about it.

Step 3: improve the date

Now the work starts. In order of how much difference each makes.

Check every post your account offers you

The most underused move available and it is free. Intra-country gaps are enormous: in August 2026 Australia ran from about 15 days at Perth to about 435 at Sydney, India from about 60 days at Kolkata to about 315 at Hyderabad, and Mexico from about 15 days at Nogales to about 180 at Guadalajara. Which posts you may select is decided by your scheduling account rather than by preference, so look rather than assume. Detail by country in India, Australia, Latin America and Nigeria.

Widen the range of dates you will accept

The biggest lever you fully control. Hunting one specific week is a fundamentally harder problem than accepting anything inside a two month band, and the difference in how often something lands is large. If your plans have any give in them at all, this is where to spend it.

Understand your reschedule allowance before you use it

Set per country by the platform. India has allowed one change per application fee since 1 January 2025; some countries allow three, some six. Your account displays your remaining attempts and that number overrides every article. With one attempt you are not looking for any earlier date, you are looking for a good one. The full rules are in can you reschedule a US visa appointment to an earlier date.

Work the calendar for cancellations

At busy posts, most earlier dates come from other applicants releasing theirs, and those arrive continuously rather than on a schedule. A few deliberate checks a day is a sustainable rhythm; continuous refreshing triggers temporary account restrictions, commonly lasting around 24 to 72 hours. A small number of missions publish their release window, which changes everything for their applicants. See when do US visa appointment slots actually open.

There is no hidden inventory

Everything in this section works on one public calendar, and it is the same one in your account. There is no reserved allocation, no partner channel and no priority queue that a fee unlocks. Any service that gets you an earlier date got it from the availability you were also looking at, by being there when it appeared. That is a real service and it is worth paying for in some circumstances, but it is attention rather than access, and anybody implying otherwise has told you something important about themselves.

Step 4: the accelerated routes

The expedited appointment request, free

The strongest option available to anyone who qualifies, and narrower than most people expect. Three categories carry: urgent medical care, supported by a letter from your doctor and a letter from the treating physician or hospital in the United States; the funeral or repatriation of an immediate family member, defined as mother, father, brother, sister or child, in the United States; and students or exchange visitors within 60 days of a programme start date, with an original I-20 or DS-2019 and SEVIS fee evidence.

Explicitly named as not qualifying: weddings, graduation ceremonies, assisting a pregnant relative, annual business or academic conferences, and last-minute tourism. The full detail, including how to write the request, is in US visa emergency appointments.

The $750 paid expedite, at two missions

The State Department's pilot lets B-1 and B-2 applicants pay $750 for an interview within ten business days. As of 19 August 2026 it runs at Mission Mexico and Mission Canada and nowhere else. It is per applicant, non-refundable, paid to the US government inside your own scheduling account, and scheduled to close on 31 December 2026. See the $750 expedited appointment explained and what changed when Canada was added.

The interview waiver, if you are one of the few still eligible

Cut twice in 2025. What remains is diplomatic and official categories, and B-1/B-2 or border crossing card renewals where the previous full validity visa expired within the past 12 months and the applicant was at least 18 when it was issued. Note that it is not always faster: at several posts the waiver queue runs far longer than the interview queue. See who still qualifies for a US visa interview waiver.

Where you are allowed to apply

This changed in 2025 and a great deal of older advice has not caught up.

Since 6 September 2025, updated on 10 October 2025, the State Department directs nonimmigrant visa applicants to schedule at the post in their country of nationality or legal residence. It is guidance rather than an outright prohibition, and existing third country appointments were generally not cancelled, but applicants who schedule elsewhere are warned they may find it more difficult to qualify, and fees are neither refunded nor transferred between posts.

The practical effect: flying to the UAE, Canada, Mexico or Thailand to escape a queue at home is no longer sensible. Applicants applying where they live rather than where they hold nationality must be prepared to demonstrate that residence. The whole picture, including who is still legitimately covered, is in can you apply for a US visa in another country.

The legitimate version of the same idea is a domestic one. Moving to a quieter post inside your own country is entirely within the guidance, and the gaps are larger than the ones people used to fly for. Start with the wait-time guide for your region.

Interview day, and after

Two things decide whether interview day goes smoothly, and only one of them is documents.

The door rules. Electronic devices and large bags are prohibited at US embassies and consulates, and there is no facility to store them. Arriving with a phone or a backpack can mean being refused entry and losing the appointment. Decide where your phone is going before you leave home. See what to bring to your US visa interview.

What happens afterwards. The published wait time measures the interview queue and nothing else. Administrative processing, where it applies, is unpredictable and outside anyone's control, and passport return sits after it. Book travel when the visa is physically in your passport, never against an interview date. The whole sequence is in the US visa timeline.

The seven mistakes that cost the most

  1. Not booking while you research. The calendar does not improve while you read, and nothing else works without a confirmed appointment.
  2. Spending a reschedule attempt on a small gain. Especially in countries that allow one. Decide in advance what improvement is worth it.
  3. Cancelling a confirmed appointment to reset something. It restores nothing and removes the one asset you hold.
  4. Refreshing continuously. It triggers temporary restrictions of roughly 24 to 72 hours, which costs you more checking time than it gained.
  5. Only ever checking the nearest post. The largest free improvement available, skipped by almost everybody.
  6. Booking travel against an interview date. Administrative processing does not care about your tickets.
  7. Paying somebody who will not say how the work is done. In March 2025 about 2,000 appointments in India were cancelled and the applicants' scheduling privileges suspended after being identified as booked by automated means. The consequences land on your account. See how to avoid US visa appointment scams.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first thing to do about a US visa appointment?

Complete the DS-160, pay the application fee, and take whatever interview date the system offers you, however distant. A confirmed appointment is the asset everything else depends on: you cannot reschedule what you do not hold, and you cannot make an expedite request without an existing booking. Waiting for a better date before booking is the most common self-inflicted delay in this process.

How do I get an earlier US visa appointment?

Four things, in order of how much difference they make. Check every consular post your scheduling account offers you, because gaps inside one country can run to hundreds of days. Widen the range of dates you will accept as far as your plans allow. Work the calendar for cancellations, which is where most earlier dates come from. And make the free expedited appointment request if you meet the documented urgency criteria.

Which website do I use to book a US visa appointment?

It depends on your country. India, Canada and many others use the AIS portal at ais.usvisa-info.com, while the UAE and others moved to usvisascheduling.com in 2025. Reach the correct one by following the link from your own embassy or consulate's website, both because it is the right platform for your country and because look-alike domains are a live problem in this market.

How many times can I reschedule a US visa appointment?

It is set per country by the scheduling platform, and the range is wide. India has allowed one change per application fee since 1 January 2025, while some countries allow three and some six. Your own account shows your remaining attempts, and that number overrides any article. Once they are exhausted, a new application fee is required before you can change anything.

What does a US visa actually cost in 2026?

For a B-1/B-2, the application fee is $185 and the $250 visa integrity fee is charged on issuance where a post is collecting it, so $435 per applicant. Petition-based categories pay $205 for the application fee. Some nationalities also owe a reciprocity fee. Nothing has a family rate, so a party of four is $1,740 before travel.

Can I apply in a different country to get a faster interview?

In almost all cases you should not. Since the State Department's guidance of 6 September 2025, updated on 10 October 2025, applicants are directed to schedule at the post in their country of nationality or legal residence, and those who apply elsewhere are warned they may find it more difficult to qualify. Fees are neither refunded nor transferred. The legitimate version of the same idea is a quieter post inside your own country.

Is there any way to pay for a faster US visa interview?

One, and it is available almost nowhere. The State Department's expedited appointment pilot lets B-1 and B-2 applicants pay $750 for an interview within ten business days, and as of 19 August 2026 it runs only at Mission Mexico and Mission Canada. It is per applicant, non-refundable, paid to the US government inside your own scheduling account, and scheduled to end on 31 December 2026.

Does rescheduling hurt my visa application?

No. Rescheduling is an administrative action in a scheduling system and has no bearing on how a consular officer assesses your eligibility. The only way it can hurt you is indirectly, by using up a limited number of attempts and leaving you unable to make a change you need later.

The bottom line

The process is not complicated. It is slow in a small number of places and quick almost everywhere else, and the confusion comes from advice written for one country being read in another.

So: submit a clean DS-160, pay the fee, and take any date. Then find out how many reschedules your country allows, check every post your account offers you, widen the range of dates you would accept, and check the calendar on a sensible rhythm. If you have a documented emergency, make the free expedite request. If you are a B-1/B-2 applicant in Mexico or Canada with a hard deadline, price the $750 lane.

Then book travel when the visa is in your passport, and not before. That last sentence has saved more money than everything above it.

What nobody can promise you

Nobody outside the US government can guarantee an interview slot, a timeframe, or a visa decision. We cannot either, and we will not pretend otherwise to win your business. Appointment availability is controlled by the consulate and adjudication by a consular officer, and no article, service or fee changes either of those. What we offer is that the work gets done properly and that you pay only if it produces a result.

The $750 Paid Expedite Is Now Live in Canada: Who It Actually Helps

Mission Canada is the second mission in the world to switch the paid lane on, and the first with a visitor visa queue measured in years. Ten business days for $750 changes the arithmetic, but for a narrower group of people than the headlines suggest.

Short answer

Yes. As of 18 August 2026 you can pay $750 for a US visitor visa interview within ten business days at any US post in Canada. Mission Canada is the second mission in the world to be switched on, after Mexico. The fee covers B-1 and B-2 applicants only, sits on top of the $185 MRV fee, is non-refundable, and buys a place in the interview queue rather than any advantage in the decision. The pilot ends on 31 December 2026.

What changed on 18 August 2026

On 9 June 2026 the State Department published a temporary final rule creating an optional expedited interview fee for visitor visa applicants. From 1 July 2026, at participating posts, a B-1 or B-2 applicant could pay $750 on top of the standard application fee and be offered an interview within ten business days.

The word doing the work in that sentence is participating. The rule authorised the fee everywhere and switched it on nowhere. Posts have to be designated one at a time. For the first four weeks of the pilot exactly one mission was designated: Mission Mexico, announced on 22 July 2026. Every write-up you read in July, including our own guide to the $750 fee, ended at the same place: useful if you are applying in Mexico, irrelevant if you are not.

On 18 August 2026 that changed. The State Department's pilot page lists Mission Canada, the Embassy and all consulates, as participating from that date. If you are applying for a visitor visa in Canada, this is the first time the US government has offered you a straightforward paid lane to an earlier interview rather than a discretionary emergency process you have to argue your way into.

Participating posts are added one mission at a time and the list is the only thing that decides whether any of this applies to you. We check it and restate the date in this article whenever it moves. If you are reading this and the official list has changed again, tell us and we will update the page.

Why this matters more in Canada than it did in Mexico

The pilot's first month was, quietly, a weak test. Mexico's visitor visa queues are long by ordinary standards but they are not the worst in the world, and a paid lane saves a Mexican applicant months rather than years.

Canada is a different proposition. These were the interview waits for B-1 and B-2 applicants at the three Canadian posts published in the State Department's own wait time data, as summarised on 18 June 2026:

Post B-1/B-2 interview wait What $750 replaces it with
Toronto 21 months An interview within 10 business days, roughly two calendar weeks, when a paid slot is open
Ottawa 14 months
Vancouver 12 months

Wait times as summarised from State Department data on 18 June 2026 by BAL Immigration News. These figures move every few weeks and Montreal, Calgary, Halifax and Quebec City are not always published. Check the official wait time tool for your post before you decide anything.

Twenty-one months against ten business days is not a convenience upgrade. For a Toronto applicant it is the difference between a trip that happens and a trip that does not. That is why this designation matters far more than the first one, and also why the capacity question below is the part to think hardest about.

Who in Canada actually needs a B visa at all

This is the filter almost every article about the pilot skips, and in Canada it decides who the news is even for.

Canadian citizens do not need a B-1/B-2 visa. They present a Canadian passport or an approved alternative document at the border or at preclearance, and are inspected and admitted as a business or tourist visitor by US Customs and Border Protection. No interview, no MRV fee, and nothing here applies to them.

So who is sitting in that 21 month Toronto queue? Almost entirely people who live in Canada without holding Canadian citizenship:

  • Permanent residents of Canada who are citizens of a country that is not visa exempt. Canadian PR status carries no US visa exemption of its own. Your citizenship decides it.
  • Foreign nationals in Canada on a work permit, most visibly the large Indian, Nigerian, Filipino and Chinese communities in the Greater Toronto Area.
  • International students on a study permit who want to visit the US during a break.
  • Visitors and family members already in Canada on temporary status.

That is worth stating plainly because it reframes the whole decision. The typical person this affects is not a wealthy tourist choosing to skip a line. It is often a family on a work permit trying to attend a wedding, a graduation or a funeral, weighing $935 a head against a queue that outlasts their permit. Which is exactly why the arithmetic further down deserves a cold look rather than a relieved one.

Who is shut out, including the fly-to-Toronto plan

Three filters apply at once and you have to clear all three.

  1. Visa category. B-1 and B-2 only. F-1 students, H-1B and L-1 workers, J-1 exchange visitors, TN professionals and every other nonimmigrant category are excluded. If you are a student in Canada looking at a programme start date, the pilot does nothing for you.
  2. Post. You have to be applying at a designated post. Since 18 August 2026 every Canadian post qualifies, which was not true the week before.
  3. Capacity. Paid slots are released in limited daily quantities and the pilot as a whole is sized at roughly 25,000 requests worldwide. Paying is not the same as being scheduled, and the option only appears if a slot is genuinely open.

The fly-to-Toronto plan does not work

Within hours of the Canada designation, the obvious idea starts circulating: if the queue in my country is impossible, I will book in Toronto and pay the $750. It is worth explaining why that fails, because the money involved is not small.

Since the State Department's guidance of 6 September 2025, updated on 10 October 2025, nonimmigrant visa applicants are directed to schedule their interview in their country of nationality or legal residence. Applicants who schedule outside it are told to expect significantly longer waits and that qualifying may be harder, and fees paid are neither refunded nor transferred. Third country processing in Canada, once a well worn route for applicants from India and elsewhere, was narrowed sharply by that guidance.

Layer the expedite fee on top and the exposure is $935, non-refundable, spent on an appointment you were not supposed to schedule there. The $750 buys an earlier interview. It does not buy the right to interview in a country you do not live in.

A warning worth taking seriously

A real, widely reported, government-run paid lane is close to a perfect setup for fraud. The fee is paid to the US government inside the official scheduling account, never to an intermediary's wallet. If anyone offers to sell you a "$750 expedited appointment" for a post that is not on the participating list, or quotes $500, or $900, or "$750 plus our handling fee" to a personal account, that is the end of the conversation. Nobody outside the government can reserve a paid expedite slot on your behalf before you have an account, a DS-160 and a paid MRV fee of your own.

How to request it, step by step

The order matters, and it catches people out. You cannot buy the expedite first and sort the paperwork afterwards.

  1. Complete your DS-160 and pay the $185 MRV fee as normal.
  2. Schedule a regular interview appointment. Whatever date the system gives you, even if it is deep into next year, take it. This is the gate: the paid option is an upgrade to an existing booking, not a way to book from scratch.
  3. Sign in to your scheduling account and click Continue.
  4. Select Request Expedite, then the Paid Expedite option. It only appears if your post participates, you meet the criteria, and an expedited appointment is actually available.
  5. Pick a date and time from what is offered, and complete the payment promptly. The slot is not held while you think about it, and failing to complete payment forfeits it.

An existing appointment with an unexpired MRV receipt counts. You do not have to start over or pay the $185 again to use the paid lane, as long as your fee receipt is still valid.

Step 2 is where most people get stuck. If the portal will not give you a regular appointment at all, or you have already used your reschedule attempts, the paid lane is not your problem yet. Start with how rescheduling to an earlier date actually works.

Why the option may not appear

Expect this. A designated mission means the feature is enabled, not that a slot is waiting for you. Common reasons the Paid Expedite option is missing:

  • No paid slots left today. Posts release a limited number of expedited appointments and Toronto has the deepest backlog in the country pushing against them. An empty morning is not a system fault.
  • You have not booked a regular appointment yet. The upgrade has nothing to attach to.
  • Your category is not B-1 or B-2. The option is not displayed at all.
  • Your existing appointment is already inside the window. There is nothing to expedite.
  • The pilot has moved on. It is scheduled to close on 31 December 2026, and the worldwide cap of roughly 25,000 requests is shared across every participating mission, not allocated per country.

That last point deserves emphasis for Canadian applicants, because it is the one nobody mentions. Canada joined a pilot that had already been running for seven weeks and is capped in total, not per mission. Whatever share of those 25,000 requests remains is now being drawn down by two missions instead of one, and Canada has the longer queues of the two. If the option shows up in your account and the date works, treating it as something you can come back to next month is a real risk.

Is it worth $750? A decision test

Run these four in order. It takes two minutes and it is cheaper than the alternative.

  1. Multiply by your party. The fee is per applicant. A family of four is $3,000 in expedite fees plus $740 in MRV fees, which is $3,740 before a flight is booked. There is no family rate and no discount for children.
  2. Check the date you actually need. If your trip is fourteen months out and your Toronto interview is twenty-one months out, you need seven months, not ten business days. Rescheduling routinely produces movement of that size. Paying $750 for speed you do not need is the most common way this fee gets wasted.
  3. Ask whether the interview is the real constraint. If your case is likely to go into administrative processing after the interview, the $750 moves the interview forward and then you join the same queue as everyone else. It compresses one stage of a two stage problem.
  4. Be honest about the application itself. Payment gives no advantage in adjudication. A weak application interviewed in ten days is a weak application, refused eleven months sooner and $750 lighter.

If you clear all four, meaning a hard deadline inside a few weeks, a small party, a straightforward case and a B-1/B-2 category, the paid lane is genuinely the best thing available to you in Canada right now, and you should use it before the capacity or the calendar runs out. Most people do not clear all four.

Frequently asked questions

Is the $750 paid expedite available in Canada?

Yes. Mission Canada joined the State Department's nonimmigrant visa expedited appointment pilot on 18 August 2026, which covers the US Embassy in Ottawa and every US consulate in Canada. Before that date the only participating mission in the world was Mission Mexico. The pilot is scheduled to end on 31 December 2026 unless the State Department extends it.

Which Canadian posts offer it?

The designation was made at mission level, so it applies to the Embassy in Ottawa and the consulates in Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver, Calgary, Halifax and Quebec City. That is not the same as a paid slot being free at your post on the day you look. Each post releases a limited number of expedited appointments, so the option appears only when one of them is actually open.

Can I pay $750 if I am applying for an F-1 or H-1B visa in Canada?

No. The pilot covers B-1 and B-2 visitor visas only. Students, workers, exchange visitors and every other nonimmigrant category are outside it, and no fee will unlock a faster interview for them. If you hold one of those categories, rescheduling within the normal system is still the only route to an earlier date.

I live in India. Can I book a Canadian appointment and pay the $750?

In practice, no. Since the State Department's September 2025 guidance, nonimmigrant visa applicants are directed to interview in their country of nationality or legal residence, and applicants who schedule elsewhere are told to expect longer waits and a harder case. Paying the Canadian expedite fee does not change where you are supposed to apply, and the fee is not refunded if the plan fails.

Is the $750 refundable if I do not attend?

No. The expedited appointment fee is non-refundable and non-transferable. If you cancel the expedited appointment, or fail to attend it, or do not complete the payment in time, you forfeit both the slot and the fee. It is separate from the $185 MRV application fee, which you pay either way.

Does paying $750 make my visa more likely to be approved?

No. The temporary final rule that created the fee states plainly that payment gives no advantage in adjudication. The same consular officer applies the same standard to the same evidence, and if your case goes into administrative processing afterwards you wait exactly as long as anyone else. The fee buys a place in the interview queue and nothing else.

Check it against the source before you pay anything

Everything on this page about the pilot comes from one official page, and you should read it yourself rather than take our word for it. The State Department maintains the participating post list there, adds missions to it one at a time, and it is the only place the list is authoritative:

US Department of State, Nonimmigrant Visa Expedited Appointment Pilot Program
travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/niv-visa-expedited-appointment-pilot-program.html

That page carries the participating post list with the date each mission joined, the fee, the eligibility criteria and the request steps. Mission Canada appears on it with a start date of 18 August 2026. If it ever stops listing Canada, or lists a post this article does not mention, the official page is right and we are out of date, so tell us and we will correct this page.

Two things to do while you are there. Check the participating list for the post you are actually applying at, because a mission being designated is not the same as a slot being free today. And check the current wait at your post on the official wait time tool, because the whole $750 question is what you are buying your way out of.

The bottom line

Canada spent 2026 with the longest visitor visa queues in the developed world and no paid way out of them. As of 18 August 2026 there is one. For a B-1/B-2 applicant in Canada with a real deadline inside the next few weeks, $750 to convert a twenty-one month Toronto wait into ten business days is, on any honest reading, good value, and the pilot closing on 31 December 2026 means the window is finite.

For everyone else, the picture is less flattering. It is per person, so a family pays thousands. It is non-refundable, so a cancelled trip is money gone. It is B-1/B-2 only, so most students and workers are outside it. It does nothing for administrative processing, nothing for your chances of approval, and nothing at all if the paid slots at your post are already taken that day. And it does not create a way to interview in Canada if you do not live in Canada.

If you are in that larger group, the older answer is still the working one: get any appointment, then move it. That is unglamorous, it takes persistence, and it is what most earlier dates in Canada are actually made of.

What nobody can promise you

Nobody outside the US government can guarantee an interview slot, a timeframe, or a visa decision. Not us, and not the $750 fee either: it commits a post to offering an appointment within ten business days when one is available, and takes your money whether or not the interview then goes your way. We will not pretend otherwise to win your business. What we offer is that the work gets done properly, that you pay only if it produces a result.

US Visa Appointment Wait Times in Australia: Sydney's Backlog and the Post Nobody Checks

Sydney carries one of the longest visitor visa queues in the developed world. Perth, in the same country under the same rules, was booking inside a fortnight. Most applicants never look at the second one.

Short answer

In August 2026 the interview-required US visitor visa wait was about 435 days at Sydney, about 165 days at Melbourne and about 15 days at Perth. Same country, same rules, same portal, and roughly twenty nine times apart end to end. Student and petition-based categories were inside a month at all three posts, so the headline number applies to visitor visas only.

Two observations before anything else. The Australian problem is a visitor visa problem specifically: every other category was inside a month at every post. And Perth at about 15 days was, in that snapshot, one of the fastest posts anywhere in the world for a visitor visa, sitting in the same range as Abuja and Recife while Sydney sat alongside Toronto.

Who is actually in the Australian visitor queue

This filter reframes the whole page and almost nothing written about Australian waits mentions it.

Australian citizens do not generally need a B-1/B-2 visa. Australia has been in the Visa Waiver Program since 1996, so an Australian passport holder travelling for tourism or business for up to 90 days applies for an ESTA and never joins this queue.

So who is sitting in a 435 day line in Sydney?

  • Non-citizens living in Australia whose own nationality is not visa exempt: permanent residents, skilled visa holders, students and their families. Australian PR carries no US visa exemption of its own. Your citizenship decides it.
  • Australians who cannot use the waiver, which is a larger group than people assume: a previous US visa refusal, a prior overstay, certain arrest or criminal history, dual nationality with particular countries, or travel to certain countries after 2011 can all take you out of ESTA and into this queue.
  • Anyone travelling for a purpose the waiver does not cover, or staying beyond 90 days.

That matters because it changes who this page is for. The typical person facing a 435 day Sydney wait is not a tourist choosing a slow option. It is frequently someone who has no alternative route at all, which is exactly why the gap to Perth is worth taking seriously.

Why Sydney and Perth are twenty nine times apart

Nothing in the rules differs. Same platform, same fee, same DS-160, same reschedule behaviour. A wait time is capacity divided by demand, and in Australia the demand is concentrated far more sharply than the capacity is.

Sydney is the country's largest consular operation and the default for the largest population centre. Melbourne takes the second share. Perth serves a smaller catchment on the other side of the continent, and the number of applicants who consider flying five hours for an interview is small.

The concentration feeds itself. Applicants pick the nearest post, that post gets longer, and the quiet one stays quiet because being quiet is not advertised. Perth is fast partly because almost nobody looks at it.

A 15 day queue is a snapshot, not a promise

Perth is fast because it is small, and small posts move fast in both directions. A modest surge in applications, or a single staffing change, moves a 15 day queue much further in percentage terms than it moves a 435 day one. Check the official tool the day you plan to act, not the number in this article, and do not buy a non-refundable domestic flight before you hold a confirmed appointment.

Using Perth: what to check before you book a flight

The move is legitimate, it is entirely inside the State Department's country of residence guidance because you are still applying where you live, and it is the largest free improvement available to most readers of this page. Four checks first.

  1. Does your account offer the post? Which Australian posts you may select is decided by your scheduling account, not by preference. Log in and look before planning anything.
  2. Can you actually attend? An interview is in person. Sydney to Perth is a five hour flight and a time zone, so it is realistically a night away. Melbourne at about 165 days may be the better trade for some readers than Perth at about 15.
  3. Have you booked something first? Take a confirmed appointment at whichever post will give you one, then improve on it. Holding nothing while you research is how people lose two months.
  4. How many reschedules do you have? Allowances are set per country, and a change that fails still risks consuming one. Know the number in your own account before you start moving things.

Before you move a confirmed appointment anywhere, check what it costs you. Reschedule allowances are set per country and running out is expensive. Read can you reschedule a US visa appointment to an earlier date.

What is not available in Australia

The $750 paid expedite

The State Department's expedited appointment pilot lets B-1/B-2 applicants pay $750 for an interview within ten business days, but only at designated missions. As of 19 August 2026 that means Mission Mexico and Mission Canada. No Australian post participates. The department has said further locations may be added while the pilot runs, and it is scheduled to close on 31 December 2026.

Anyone offering to sell you a $750 fast track in Sydney is selling something that does not exist there.

Leaving the country for a shorter queue

Flying to New Zealand, Singapore or elsewhere to escape the Sydney queue was a real strategy once. Since the guidance of 6 September 2025, updated 10 October 2025, applicants are directed to schedule in their country of nationality or legal residence, and those who go elsewhere are warned they may find it harder to qualify, with fees neither refunded nor transferred.

Perth is the legitimate version of that idea. Same benefit, domestic flight, no warning attached.

What is available

The ordinary expedited appointment request: free, discretionary, decided by the consular section on documented urgency. Medical emergencies, the funeral of an immediate family member, unavoidable urgent business travel, documented student start dates. Book a regular appointment first, because the request attaches to an existing booking, and keep that appointment until a decision comes back.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the US visa wait in Australia?

It depends entirely on which post you use. In August 2026 the interview-required B-1/B-2 wait was about 435 days at Sydney, about 165 days at Melbourne and about 15 days at Perth. That is a spread of roughly 29 to 1 inside one country, on the same rules, and it is the single most useful fact for anyone applying in Australia.

Why is Sydney's US visa wait so long?

Sydney is the largest consular section in the country and absorbs most of the national demand, and a wait time is simply capacity divided by demand. The concentration is partly self-reinforcing: applicants default to the post in the biggest city, which keeps Sydney loaded and the other posts comparatively quiet. Nothing about Sydney's rules or process is different, only its queue.

Do Australians need a US visa at all?

Usually not. Australia has been in the Visa Waiver Program since 1996, so Australian citizens travelling for tourism or business for up to 90 days apply for an ESTA rather than a visa. That is why the B-1/B-2 queue in Australia is made up mostly of non-citizens living in Australia, and of Australians who are outside the waiver because of a previous refusal, a prior overstay, certain travel history or other ineligibility.

Can I apply in Perth if I live in Sydney?

Often yes, and it is the largest free improvement available to an Australian applicant, but it is governed by what your scheduling account actually offers you rather than by preference. Check the posts available in your account before planning a flight, and remember you have to attend in person, so weigh the domestic travel and a night away against the months saved.

Are student and work visa waits shorter in Australia?

Dramatically. In August 2026 the F, M and J wait was about 45 days at Sydney and about 30 days at Melbourne and Perth, and petition-based H, L, O, P and Q cases were around 30 days at all three. Consular sections prioritise categories with fixed start dates. If you are a student or a sponsored worker, the visitor visa headline number is not your queue.

Can I pay $750 for a faster interview in Australia?

No. The State Department's expedited appointment pilot runs only at designated missions, and as of 19 August 2026 those are Mission Mexico and Mission Canada. No Australian post participates, so no fee will buy a faster interview in Sydney, Melbourne or Perth. The free expedite request on documented urgency is the only accelerated route available there.

Should I fly to New Zealand or Asia for a shorter queue instead?

No. Since the State Department's guidance of 6 September 2025, updated on 10 October 2025, applicants are directed to schedule in their country of nationality or legal residence, and those applying elsewhere are warned they may find it harder to qualify. Fees are neither refunded nor transferred. If you live in Australia, Perth is the legitimate version of that idea and it is a domestic flight rather than an international one.

The bottom line

Australia does not have a US visa wait time. It has three, and in August 2026 they ran from about 15 days to about 435. Before you accept the Sydney number as your fate, find out which posts your scheduling account will actually offer you.

Understand also that this is a visitor visa problem and not a general one. Students, exchange visitors and sponsored workers were inside a month at every Australian post, so if that is you, the frightening figure at the top of this page is not your queue.

And if you are in the Sydney visitor queue with no alternative route, the honest options are a flight to another Australian post, a documented expedite request if you genuinely qualify, and steady work on the calendar. There is no paid government fast lane here, and leaving the country stopped being sensible in September 2025.

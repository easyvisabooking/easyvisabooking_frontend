US Visa Appointment Wait Times in Nigeria: Among the Shortest in the World

The assumption is that West African applicants wait longest. In August 2026 Abuja was booking visitor interviews inside about two weeks while Toronto sat past sixteen months, and almost nobody has noticed the inversion.

Short answer

In August 2026 the interview-required US visitor visa wait was about 15 days at Abuja and about 30 days at Lagos. Those are among the shortest queues in the world. Toronto sat near 495 days, Sydney near 435 and Hyderabad near 315 in the same snapshot. If a Nigerian application is taking months, the appointment calendar is almost certainly not the reason.

How the world map inverted

For years the intuitive model was that visa queues track wealth: applicants from poorer countries wait, applicants from rich ones do not. That model is now wrong, and Nigeria is the clearest illustration of why.

Two things drive it. Staffing is allocated post by post and does not track population or demand smoothly, so a post that is adequately staffed for its caseload clears quickly whatever the country's income level. And demand shifted: the visitor visa queues that exploded are in countries with large non-citizen resident populations, because a permanent resident of Canada or Australia holding a non-exempt passport still needs a US visa. Canada and Australia both send their own citizens to the United States on visa-free travel, so their B-1/B-2 queues are made up almost entirely of foreign nationals living there.

Nigeria has no such structure. The people in the Nigerian queue are Nigerian, applying at home, in a post whose capacity currently matches its caseload. The result is a two week queue in a country most commentary still describes as slow.

A short queue is not the same as an easy application

We are not going to pretend that a 15 day appointment wait means a straightforward process, because for Nigerian applicants it frequently does not. Refusal rates, documentary expectations and administrative processing are separate questions from the calendar, and they are the ones that decide most outcomes here. This page is about one stage of three, and it is the stage that happens to be working.

If the queue is short, what is taking so long?

Applicants in Nigeria routinely describe a process running for months, which sounds incompatible with a two week appointment queue. Both things are true, because the published figure measures one stage only.

Stage Is it in the published wait time? Who controls it
Getting an interview appointment Yes. This is the whole figure Post capacity, and how early you book
The interview and decision No A consular officer, on your evidence
Administrative processing (221g) No Nobody you can reach. Unpredictable in length
Passport return and delivery No The courier and collection arrangements at your post

The State Department states that published wait times do not include time required for administrative processing. Retrieved 19 August 2026.

So the practical advice for a Nigerian applicant runs opposite to the advice for a Canadian one. In Toronto the whole game is the calendar. In Abuja the calendar is close to solved, and the leverage is all in the application: complete and consistent documentation, a DS-160 with no errors that will need correcting, and enough margin after the interview to absorb administrative processing without losing a booked trip.

The rest of West Africa

Nigeria is quick, but the region is not uniform. Accra was around 105 days for visitor interviews in August 2026, several times the Nigerian queue, with a very short interview waiver route alongside it for those eligible.

That matters mainly as a warning against a plan that used to circulate: applying at a neighbouring post to skip a queue. Since the State Department's guidance of 6 September 2025, updated on 10 October 2025, applicants are directed to apply in their country of nationality or legal residence, and those who go elsewhere are warned they may find it harder to qualify, with fees neither refunded nor transferred. For a Nigerian applicant with a two week queue at home, the idea has nothing to recommend it in either direction.

Thinking about applying somewhere else anyway? The rules changed in September 2025 and the money does not follow you. Read can you apply for a US visa in another country before you book a flight.

What to do with a short queue

The right strategy in a fast market is almost the reverse of the one in a slow one.

  1. Do not rush the DS-160 to reach the calendar sooner. When the queue is two weeks, there is nothing to race towards, and an error that forces a new form and a new confirmation number costs more than the wait ever would.
  2. Book once and keep it. With queues this short there is little to gain from moving an appointment, and each change carries its own risk.
  3. Spend the effort on the file, not the calendar. Consistent documents, a clear purpose of travel, and evidence that matches what the DS-160 says.
  4. Leave margin after the interview. Administrative processing is the unpredictable stage, and no amount of early booking shortens it. Do not buy non-refundable travel against an interview date.
  5. Use the free expedite request only if you genuinely qualify, on documented medical, funeral or student start date grounds. At a 15 day queue there will rarely be a reason.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the US visa appointment wait in Nigeria?

Short, by world standards. In August 2026 the interview-required B-1/B-2 wait was about 15 days at Abuja and about 30 days at Lagos. Student, petition-based and crew categories were around 15 days at both posts. For comparison, Toronto was near 495 days and Sydney near 435 in the same snapshot.

Is Abuja or Lagos faster for a US visa interview?

In August 2026 Abuja was the faster of the two for visitor visas, at about 15 days against about 30 at Lagos, and both were quick in absolute terms. At queues this short the difference is rarely worth travelling for, and which post you may use is decided by your scheduling account rather than by preference.

If the wait is short, why does my US visa still take months?

Because the appointment calendar is only the first of three stages, and in Nigeria it is not the binding one. After the interview a case can go into administrative processing, which is unpredictable and outside anyone's control. Passport return and delivery adds more. A published wait time measures the distance to the interview and nothing after it.

Can I use the $750 paid expedite in Nigeria?

No. The State Department's expedited appointment pilot runs only at designated missions, and as of 19 August 2026 those are Mission Mexico and Mission Canada. No Nigerian post participates. Given queues of about 15 to 30 days, there would be very little for it to buy in any case.

Should I apply in Ghana or another neighbouring country instead?

No, and it would be slower as well as riskier. Accra was around 105 days for visitor interviews in August 2026, several times Abuja's queue. More importantly, since the State Department's guidance of 6 September 2025, updated on 10 October 2025, applicants are directed to apply in their country of nationality or legal residence, and fees are neither refunded nor transferred if the plan fails.

Do these figures apply to student visas?

Student and exchange categories were around 15 days at both Nigerian posts in the same August 2026 snapshot, so the picture is similar. If your programme start date is close, the free expedited appointment request also exists for students within 60 days of a start date, with an original I-20 or DS-2019 and SEVIS fee evidence.

Do I need a paid service to get an appointment in Nigeria?

Almost certainly not, and we would rather say so than take the money. At a two to four week queue there is very little for a rescheduling service to improve. Book the first available appointment, prepare the application properly, and spend nothing. The value of a service like ours is in queues measured in hundreds of days, which is not what Nigeria has.

The bottom line

The received wisdom about US visa waits in West Africa is roughly a decade out of date. In August 2026 Abuja was about 15 days and Lagos about 30, against 495 in Toronto and 435 in Sydney. The longest visitor visa queues in the world are now in wealthy English-speaking countries with large non-citizen populations, not in Nigeria.

The consequence for applicants is a different set of priorities. The calendar is not your problem, so do not spend money or anxiety on it. The application file and the unpredictable stretch of administrative processing after the interview are where the risk actually sits, and neither of those responds to booking earlier.

Book once, prepare properly, and leave margin before any travel you cannot change.

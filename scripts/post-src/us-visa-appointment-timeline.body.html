The shape of that table is the whole point. One stage dominates the timeline and is publicly measured. One stage is unpredictable and invisible. And the two in between are short. Any plan built on the published number alone is a plan that has ignored stage four.

The stage nobody can price

Administrative processing is additional review after the interview. It is applied case by case, it is not explained to the applicant in detail, and there is no meaningful way to influence or accelerate it from outside.

What that means in planning terms:

  • No range is trustworthy. Any article giving you a typical duration is extrapolating from anecdotes. It can be days. It can be many months.
  • It is not a sign that something has gone wrong. It is a normal part of the process for some cases, and it is not the same as a refusal.
  • Nobody outside the government can shorten it. Not a service, not an agent, not a fee. If anyone tells you otherwise, that is the most useful thing they will ever tell you about themselves.
  • An earlier interview does not reduce it. It moves the start of it forward, which is worth something, but it compresses one stage of a two stage problem.

When to book flights

When the visa is physically in your passport. That is the whole answer, and it is worth the paragraph it takes to explain why the tempting alternative fails.

Booking against an interview date assumes four things at once: that the interview happens as scheduled, that the decision is favourable, that it is immediate, and that no administrative processing follows. Each can fail on its own. The interview can be cancelled for reasons at the post. The decision can be a refusal. And processing can add an unpredictable stretch that no amount of preparation shortens.

If you genuinely have to commit earlier, commit to refundable or changeable arrangements and price the flexibility as part of the trip. It is almost always cheaper than the alternative.

What you actually control

Ranked by how much difference it makes.

  1. Book immediately, whatever the date. Stage two only starts when you hold an appointment, and every day spent researching before booking is a day added to the total.
  2. Check every post your account offers you. The largest single improvement available to most applicants, and it is free. Sydney to Perth was about 420 days of difference in August 2026.
  3. Widen the range of dates you will accept. The lever that decides whether persistence works.
  4. Get the DS-160 right first time. Not because errors cause refusals, but because a new form means a new confirmation number and a rebooking problem.
  5. Watch the MRV fee clock. Valid for one year from payment, and your interview must sit inside that window. At a post with a queue near or past a year, this can end your options before your appointment arrives.
  6. Make the free expedite request if you qualify. Documented medical, funeral or student start date grounds. It is the only free route that jumps the queue.

The bottom line

The number everybody quotes measures one stage of four. It is the largest stage and the only one worth working on, which is why so much advice in this subject is about it, but a plan that stops there has left out the part that actually ruins trips.

So build your timeline in this order. Days for the form and the fee. The published wait for your post, checked on the official tool rather than taken from an article. One day for the interview. And then an honest blank for administrative processing, because no credible range exists.

Book travel when the visa is in your passport. Everything else on this page is about reaching that point sooner, and none of it is about being able to predict when.

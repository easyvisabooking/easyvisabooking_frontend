Short answer

At most US consular posts in the world, an interview inside three months is the default and requires nothing special. At about a dozen busy posts it is out of reach without a granted emergency request. In August 2026 the yes list included Abuja, Recife, Perth, Nogales and Kolkata. The no list included Toronto, Sydney, Vancouver, Hyderabad, Mumbai, New Delhi and Bogota. Everything depends on which list you are on.

Notice what the right-hand column has in common. Canada and Australia are wealthy countries whose own citizens mostly travel to the United States without a visa, so their queues are made up of foreign nationals living there. Hyderabad, Mumbai and Delhi carry the world's largest single applicant population. Bogota runs an entire country through one consular section. None of it is random, and none of it is about your case.

Why three months is the official dividing line

Three months is not an arbitrary number people picked. The State Department publishes average B-1/B-2 wait times only where the next available appointment is more than three months away.

That gives you a useful shortcut when reading the official table. If your post has no published B-1/B-2 average, it is offering something inside three months. A post that does have one has told you, in the act of publishing it, that it cannot.

If you are on the yes list

Then this article is short for you, and the advice is mostly about not creating problems that do not exist.

  • Book the first available date and keep it. At a two to six week queue there is very little to gain by moving, and each change carries risk.
  • Do not rush the DS-160 to reach the calendar faster. An error that forces a new form and a new confirmation number costs more than the queue does.
  • Do not pay anybody. Not an alert plan, not a rescheduling service, not us. There is nothing meaningful for a paid option to improve.
  • Leave margin after the interview. The published wait measures the distance to the interview only. Administrative processing afterwards is unpredictable and no amount of early booking shortens it.

If you are on the no list

Then three months through ordinary scheduling is not going to happen, and being told that clearly is more useful than being sold hope. There are four honest moves.

  1. Book the distant date immediately. This is not a concession, it is the prerequisite. You cannot reschedule what you do not hold, and you cannot request an expedite without an existing booking. Every other option on this list requires this step first.
  2. Check every post your account offers you. The largest free improvement available, and almost nobody uses it. Sydney to Perth was about 435 days to about 15. Hyderabad to Kolkata was about 315 to about 60. Whether your account offers the alternative is decided by the system, so look rather than assume.
  3. Make the expedite request if you genuinely qualify. Free, discretionary, decided on documented urgency: urgent medical care with two letters, the funeral of a mother, father, brother, sister or child in the United States, or a student within 60 days of a programme start with the original I-20 or DS-2019 and SEVIS fee evidence. Weddings, graduations and conferences are named as not qualifying.
  4. If you are in Mexico or Canada, price the $750 lane. It is the only fee that reliably produces an interview inside ten business days, it covers B-1 and B-2 only, it is per applicant and non-refundable, and the pilot is scheduled to close on 31 December 2026.

After that, what is left is persistence on the calendar with the widest date range your plans allow. That is unglamorous and it is what most earlier dates at busy posts are actually made of.

Three months at Toronto is not a service problem

We get asked for it, and the honest answer is that at a queue near 495 days, nobody can reliably deliver an interview inside three months, us included. Persistence improves a date, often substantially, but the size of that improvement is set by what the consulate releases and by how flexible you can be, not by who is watching. If someone promises you three months at a post like that, the promise is the product and it is not a real one.

Check your visa category before you panic

Almost every frightening number in this article is a visitor visa number, and visitor visas are the worst-served category at nearly every busy post.

In the same August 2026 snapshot: student and exchange categories were inside a month at every Australian post while Sydney's visitor queue ran past a year. At Mumbai and New Delhi, student waits were around 75 days and petition-based waits around 45, against 255 for visitors. At Bogota, petition-based cases were around 15 days against 270 for visitors.

So if you are a student, an exchange visitor or a sponsored worker, look up your own category before you make any plans on the basis of a headline figure. Three months is very often not your problem at all.

The bottom line

Three months is easy almost everywhere and impossible in about a dozen places. Look up your post before you do anything else, because the answer for Abuja and the answer for Toronto have nothing in common.

If you are on the fast list, book, keep it, and stop reading. If you are on the slow list, book anyway, then check every other post your account will offer you, then make the free expedite request if your circumstances genuinely meet the criteria. If you are in Mexico or Canada with a real deadline, the $750 lane is the one paid option that reliably works.

And check your visa category. The numbers that frighten people are visitor visa numbers, and students and sponsored workers are usually looking at a completely different queue.

Short answer

Since 2 September 2025 the interview waiver covers only diplomatic and official categories, plus B-1/B-2 and border crossing card renewals where the previous full validity visa expired within the past 12 months and the applicant was at least 18 when it was issued. H-1B, L-1, O-1 and most other categories lost it. And at several posts the waiver queue is now longer than the interview queue, so eligibility does not automatically mean speed.

What changed, in two rounds

The interview waiver, known almost everywhere as dropbox, let qualifying applicants renew a visa by submitting documents instead of attending an interview. For anyone facing a queue measured in months, it was the cleanest legitimate way past it.

It was cut twice in one year.

  • February 2025. Eligibility was narrowed to renewals in the same visa category, within 12 months of the previous visa's expiry, and first-time applicants were removed entirely. Significant, but a large number of renewing workers and students still qualified.
  • 2 September 2025. The much larger cut. The common nonimmigrant categories were taken out, including H-1B, L-1 and O-1. What was a document drop-off for a renewing worker in 2024 became an in-person interview.

The practical effect is a large population of applicants who have renewed by dropbox several times, expect to do it again, and discover at the point of booking that they are now in the interview queue. If that is you, nothing has gone wrong with your case. The rules moved.

Eligibility as set out in the 2 September 2025 changes. Retrieved 19 August 2026. Posts apply these individually and eligibility remains discretionary in every case, so follow your own post's published instructions.

Three conditions on that second row and all three have to hold. Read them one at a time:

  • Full validity. A visa issued for a limited period rather than the standard validity for your nationality does not carry the same treatment.
  • Expired within the past 12 months. Measured from expiry, not from issuance. Thirteen months and you are outside it.
  • At least 18 when it was issued. This catches families out. A visa issued to a child does not qualify that person for a waiver renewal as an adult.

The part nobody mentions: it is not always faster

Everyone treats the waiver as the fast lane, which was true when it was widely available and is no longer reliably true at all. Interview waiver processing has its own queue, and that queue is published separately from the interview queue.

In August 2026 the pattern in Mexico was striking, and it goes the wrong way:

Merida About 90 days About 333 days

As recorded from the State Department's wait time tool in August 2026. Retrieved 19 August 2026. This pattern is not universal: at Hermosillo the waiver wait was about a day, and in Brazil waiver waits were a day or two against interview waits of 15 to 75 days. The point is that you have to look, not that the waiver is always slower.

So the correct question is not "am I eligible for the waiver" but "which of the two queues at my post is shorter". The official wait time tool publishes both figures side by side for every post, and checking takes about a minute. Assuming the waiver is faster has cost applicants months.

A waiver you cannot use is worse than an interview you can

There is a version of this that goes badly. An applicant confirms they are eligible, submits documents into a queue running past 300 days, and only then discovers that the interview queue at the same post was 90. Waiver submissions are not a booking you can casually swap out of, and the time spent waiting is not recoverable. Check both numbers before you commit to a route, not after.

Eligible is not the same as granted

Meeting every condition makes you eligible to be considered. Consular officers retain discretion to require an in-person interview in any case, and they exercise it.

Plan accordingly. That means not booking non-refundable travel on the assumption that the waiver route will complete without an interview, and being ready to attend if you are called. It also means that anything materially changed since your previous visa was issued, a new employer, a different purpose of travel, a change in circumstances, makes an interview more likely rather than less.

Timing decisions the 12 month rule creates

The expiry-based window turns visa renewal into a scheduling problem, which it was not before.

  • Do not let a B-1/B-2 lapse past 12 months if you can avoid it. Once it does, you are outside the waiver and into the interview queue, and at a long-queue post that decision can cost you the better part of a year.
  • Count from expiry, not from your last trip. Applicants routinely think of the visa as expiring when they last used it.
  • Check the waiver queue before you time it. If the waiver wait at your post is 300 days, being eligible for it changes less than you think.
  • Children need their own analysis. A visa issued before 18 does not support a waiver renewal, so families should expect to book interviews for younger members even where the adults qualify.

If you are outside it now

Most readers of this page are, and the honest answer is that you are in the ordinary process with everybody else. The levers are the ordinary ones and they are worth more attention than they used to get:

  1. Book any interview date immediately. Whatever the calendar offers. Everything else depends on holding an appointment.
  2. Check every post your account offers you. Intra-country gaps are large and almost nobody uses them.
  3. Widen your acceptable date range. The single biggest free lever.
  4. Use the free expedite request if you genuinely qualify, on medical, funeral or student start date grounds with documents behind it.
  5. Then work the calendar, because cancellations are where most earlier dates actually come from.

The bottom line

The interview waiver went from a broad convenience to a narrow exception in the space of seven months. Diplomatic categories kept it. B-1/B-2 renewals kept it on three tight conditions. Almost everybody else lost it, including the employment categories that used it most.

If you are still inside it, the useful discipline is to stop treating it as automatically faster. Look up both queues for your own post, because at several posts the waiver route was running months behind the interview route in August 2026, and the assumption in the other direction is expensive.

If you are outside it, you are where most people now are: an in-person interview, an ordinary queue, and the ordinary levers. Book something today, widen your dates, check every post you may use, and work the calendar.

Short answer

In August 2026 the interview-required US visitor visa wait in Latin America ran from about 15 days at Nogales and Recife to about 270 days at Bogota. Mexico spans most of that range on its own, from about 15 days to about 180. Brazil is uniformly quick. Colombia is the regional outlier. And Mexico is the only mission in the region where the $750 paid expedite operates.

Brazil is the one place in this article where the interview waiver behaves the way everybody assumes it does: a day or two, at every post. If you are eligible for it in Brazil, it is unambiguously the right route.

Bogota, the regional outlier

Colombia runs its entire nonimmigrant caseload through one consular section, and in August 2026 the visitor queue there was about 270 days, roughly four times Sao Paulo and eighteen times Nogales.

What makes it interesting is the shape of the problem. At the same post, in the same month, student and exchange cases were around 60 days and petition-based cases around 15. That is not a post that has run out of capacity. It is a post where visitor demand has run far ahead of the share of capacity allocated to it.

For a Colombian applicant that means two things. The headline figure is not your queue if you are a student or a sponsored worker. And if you are a visitor applicant, there is no second Colombian post to move to, which is the lever available to Mexican and Brazilian applicants and not to you.

A post with no alternative is genuinely harder

Most of the advice on this site rests on checking every post you are entitled to use, and in Colombia that advice is empty: there is one. Leaving the country is not the answer either, for the reasons set out below. So the honest position for a Bogota visitor applicant is that the levers are narrower than usual: hold your appointment, widen the range of dates you will accept, and work the cancellation supply. We would rather say that than imply we have something clever for a case where nobody does.

The interview waiver trap

Applicants across the region reach for the interview waiver on the assumption it is faster. In Brazil that is right. In Mexico and Colombia it is frequently the opposite, and by a very large margin.

The rule to take away is not "the waiver is slow". It is that the two queues are independent and you have to check both. The official wait time tool publishes them side by side for every post, and it takes a minute.

Not sure you are still eligible for the waiver at all? The rules were cut twice in 2025 and most categories lost it. Read who still qualifies for a US visa interview waiver.

The $750 lane, and who can actually use it

Mission Mexico was the first mission in the world designated for the State Department's expedited appointment pilot. As of 19 August 2026 the only other one is Mission Canada. Nothing in Brazil or Colombia participates.

What it is, precisely: $750 per applicant, on top of the application fee, for an interview within ten business days when a paid slot is available. B-1 and B-2 only. Paid to the US government inside your own scheduling account, never to an intermediary. Non-refundable. Scheduled to end on 31 December 2026.

Two cautions specific to this region:

  • Check whether you need it. At a Mexican post already booking inside 45 days, $750 per person buys you a few weeks. The fee is worth considering at Guadalajara at 180 days and hard to justify at Nogales at 15.
  • It does not create a right to apply in Mexico. Applicants elsewhere in Latin America occasionally read the designation as an invitation. Since the guidance of 6 September 2025, updated 10 October 2025, you are directed to apply in your country of nationality or legal residence, and fees are neither refunded nor transferred if it goes wrong.

The bottom line

Latin America contains both ends of the global range, and the variation is mostly within countries rather than between them. Mexico spans about 15 to 180 days across ten posts. Brazil is quick everywhere. Colombia is slow in one category at one post, with no alternative to move to.

Three practical rules follow. Check every post your account offers you, because in Mexico that is worth up to five months. Check the interview waiver queue as well as the interview queue, because in Mexico and Colombia the waiver is often the slower route by hundreds of days. And check your own visa category, because the frightening numbers in this article are visitor visa numbers and almost nothing else in the region is slow.
